# BL2 — Tail Quantiles and Bounds-Form Fitting (0.2.0)

**Status:** implemented on top of 0.1.0 (`7ee3036`, Nov 2026); not yet released. These are the small items queued before the piecewise-BL port; 0.2.0 ships once that port is done. Full test suite: 505 passed, 18 skipped (Suite B).

**Epistemic labels**, as in the phase notes: *Proof* = a complete argument. *Numerical* = measured against an independent mpmath reference, or against scipy's own implementations; true for the cases tested, not a theorem.

---

## 1. Tail quantiles (Numerical)

**The problem.** Phase 3 listed `isf` as open: scipy's default is `ppf(1 − q)`. Measuring it against new reference values showed it was worse than listed, and that the lower tail had the same flaw.

- `isf(q)` was wrong by more than 100% for q below about 1e-9, at every dial position including `'exact'`.
- `ppf(q)` in the lower tail was wrong by more than 100% below about 1e-9 at the three grid positions. The body spline interpolates x against q itself, so its error is absolute in q (~1e-7 at `'balanced'`), and it drops cdf steps below 1e-12. `'exact'` was already fine, since it root-finds on the left-integrated cdf.

**The fix.**

- Grid positions: below a tail probability of 1e-3, `ppf` and `isf` are inverted from cached splines of x against log F and log S, where F and S are the cdf and sf integrated from their own ends (the same graded quadrature as `cdf` and `sf`). The error is then relative in q.
- Between 1e-4 and 1e-3 the tail and body splines are blended linearly in log q. An abrupt switch at 1e-3 let `ppf` step backwards by the two methods' disagreement (8e-7 of the scale at `'fast'`); the blend is monotone as long as they disagree by less than about 200%.
- `'exact'`: `isf` is brentq on sf, mirroring `ppf`'s brentq on cdf.
- The tail splines are built lazily, on the first tail quantile for a parameter set: about 4, 30 and 250 ms at `'fast'`, `'balanced'` and `'precise'`, then about 0.5 ms per 1000 quantiles.
- `rvs` is unchanged, bit for bit: it still inverts the body spline only. Tail accuracy is irrelevant at sampling-noise level, and it would cost the second grid build.
- Above the tails nothing changed: `isf(q)` is exactly `ppf(1 − q)` for q ≥ 1e-3, and Suites A–E pass unchanged.

**Accuracy.** Worst relative error in q over 6 cases (including the needle and mode-near-bound cases) and q from 1e-3 to 1e-30, against the reference:

- `'fast'` 3e-3
- `'balanced'` 2e-4
- `'precise'` 2e-5
- `'exact'` 6e-11

Below the effective-support cutoff (tail probability around 1e-35 to 1e-45, depending on the case) the quantile is pinned to the support edge, as `cdf` and `sf` are 0 there.

**Reference model.** A new 50-digit mpmath model of BL2, written independently of the library's numerics: bisection on the `lognorm_params` quartic, tanh-sinh quadrature over break points graded toward the mode and both bounds, quantiles by Newton on log F and log S. Before use it was checked against the existing gold values: `lognorm_params` to 4e-21, the Suite E tail sf values to 7e-19, and a Suite D edge quantile to 2e-19.

## 2. `fit_bounds` (Numerical)

**Purpose.** Phase 3's `fit` is scipy's, over (c, s, loc, scale). It cannot fix the mode alone (c depends on loc and scale), and it has no least-squares option. The original notebook's `fit` had both, and the 100m-sprints notebook uses them: `fit(MLE=False, flower=0)`, once with `fmode` too, and the least-squares covariance for a confidence interval on the fitted upper bound. `fit_bounds` restores this in the bounds form.

- `bl2.fit_bounds(data, method='MLE' | 'LSQ', fmode=, fsigma=, flower=, fupper=, gmode=, ..., ecdf='midpoint' | 'step')`
- Returns a `BoundsFit` that unpacks as `(mode, sigma, lower, upper)` and carries `.method`, `.free`, `.nnlf`, `.success`, `.message`, and for LSQ `.cov` and `.stderr`.
- Lives in `_fit.py` and uses only a distribution's public API (`bounds_to_shapes`, `logpdf`, `cdf`), so the piecewise BL can share it once ported.

**How it works.** The free parameters are optimized in unconstrained coordinates in which every point is a valid parameter set: free bounds sit outside the data and outside a fixed mode (log of the margin), the mode is a logit between the bounds, sigma a log. MLE uses Nelder–Mead; LSQ uses scipy's `least_squares`.

**Checks.**

- MLE with everything free reaches the same optimum as scipy's `fit`: equal negative log-likelihood, parameters within 1e-7.
- MLE with both bounds fixed matches scipy's `fit` with `floc`/`fscale`, at a likelihood at least as good.
- LSQ returns exactly what `scipy.optimize.curve_fit` returns on the same empirical cdf, and the covariance agrees to 3e-7.

**Two deliberate differences from the original.**

- **Empirical cdf.** The default is now the midpoint of each jump, the Hazen position (i − ½)/n. The original used the top of each jump, i/n, which puts the largest value at exactly 1 and pulls a fitted upper bound toward max(data). That bias matters for the sprints notebook's "best possible runner", which *is* the fitted upper bound. `ecdf='step'` reproduces the original.
- **No silent runaways.** If the data cannot pin a parameter down, the fit used to drift until it stopped somewhere arbitrary (sigma reached 1e145 in one test). sigma and the distance of each free bound from the data are now capped at 1000 and 10,000 times the data range. A fit that ends within a factor of 10 of a cap has `success = False` and raises a warning naming the parameter. Two cases trigger it in the tests: a fixed mode inconsistent with the data, and a free upper bound when the mode sits almost on the lower bound (a sample then says almost nothing about the upper bound).

**The LSQ covariance is curve_fit's, with curve_fit's flaw.** `.cov` is s²(JᵀJ)⁻¹ with s² = SSR/(m − p), as the original returned. It treats the empirical-cdf residuals as independent, which they are not, so its standard errors understate the real uncertainty. For a confidence interval, for example on the upper bound, refit bootstrap resamples. No covariance is offered for MLE: with a free bound the usual regularity conditions for the inverse Fisher information do not clearly hold.

## 4. Tests (mutation-checked)

- **Suite F** (53 tests): tail quantiles against the reference at every dial position (F1 isf, F2 ppf), monotonicity of both over q from 1e-40 to 0.5 with dense sampling at the blend (F3), and array shapes plus `isf(q) == ppf(1 − q)` above the tails (F4). Reverting each part of the fix fails the matching tests.
- **Suite G** (18 tests): agreement with scipy's `fit` and with `curve_fit` (G1–G3), fixed parameters held exactly (G4), a fixed mode below all the data (G4b), runaways reported as failures (G4c), recovery of the truth (G5), the two empirical-cdf conventions (G6), and rejected input (G7). A sign flip in the MLE objective, the step ECDF as default, a wrong covariance denominator, and free bounds not kept outside a fixed mode each fail the expected tests; the last is caught by G4c only, because the penalty on invalid trial points steers G4b's fit back by itself.
